# The brush mascot

Callout Studio's icon, the paintbrush, sometimes comes alive. Two eyes rise out of the stripe across the bristles, the stripe bends into a mouth, and when the moment has passed, both go back. The brush is then the plain icon again.

The brush takes your theme's grey, and the only color it ever uses is your theme's accent: for its tears, a splash of paint, a blush, or a stroke it paints. In the default theme that is purple.

## Where you'll meet it

- **Empty lists.** When **My callout types**, **Saved color palettes** or **Custom commands** has nothing in it yet, the brush waits above the message and peeks now and then. Point at the **+** and it gets excited.
- **Searches that find nothing.** In the icon picker, and in the search field of **My callout types**, **Built-in callouts** and **Callouts from your theme**, it sits above the words "No callout matches" and your search. When the search turns up nothing it looks around in three directions (down left, up left, down right), staring at each for a moment with its eyes narrowing as if it were straining to see, then shakes its head "no" and goes back to looking sad. While you keep typing it looks around again, and when you pause and there is still nothing, it shakes its head once more and is sad again. **Quick insert** does the same. **Find callouts** looks around and shakes its head once when its list turns empty, then stays sad for as long as nothing matches. In all of them there is no pause as the plain icon between the shake and the sadness.
- **No pictures yet.** In the icon picker's **Your images**, before you have added any, it waves hello, and gets excited when you drag a file over it.
- **Nothing to convert.** In **Review conversion** it nods: all good.
- **No earlier versions.** In **Restore an earlier version** it looks around.
- **Before you delete.** Deleting a callout, a palette, a command, a picture, a version or an icon library makes it nervous, and so do **Reset everything** and converting your notes. In every one of these questions the brush is the same size and in the title's colour, cries while your pointer is on the button that cannot be undone, and breathes out in relief on **Cancel** and on the **✕** in the corner. Every one of these questions has a heading of two lines: the title, and under it a line that says which one it is (the name of the item, in quotes) or what it costs. The brush stands beside both, at the edge the words start from and level with the middle of the two lines, and the message sits below them across the full width. On a phone there is no brush in these questions: a finger has no hover, so it could only look nervous, and it would take a row of a narrow screen. The two lines stay.
- **Every notice.** The small messages that pop up in the corner all have the brush beside their words. It keeps still for a moment, then moves for as long as the message is up, and what it does depends on the news, always the same for the same kind: it is happy when something worked, celebrates a bigger job, breathes out when something is back (an undo, a restored version), nods when there was nothing to do, looks around when what you asked for is not there, shakes its head when a request cannot be done as asked, is sad when something failed, startled when it only half worked, stands still while saving is paused, and waves hello for plain information.
- **While something works.** During **Scan for callouts**, a conversion, a library download, or the download of a language, it paints, starting the moment the message appears. A scan that finishes in a blink still keeps its **Scanning…** message up for at least a second, then pauses a moment before the result appears. A successful scan or conversion ends with a little celebration, and so does an import that worked.
- **Import offer.** In the first-install banner that offers to import from Callout Manager or Admonition, the brush waves hello once, in the accent color, at the start of the message.
- **Saving.** While saving is paused it stands in the orange warning banner's corner in place of the triangle, and in the corner notice, calmly, because nothing is lost. If a save failed, it is sad.
- **Small moments.** It shakes its head beside the reason when you press a dimmed button. In the welcome window, at the start of the text, it smiles and winks as the window opens, winks once more each time your pointer comes onto it, and gets angry if you press it again and again, like every other brush.

## Playing with it

The brush answers your pointer wherever it appears, except in the small messages in the corner and in the import offer banner.

- **Come near.** Move your pointer onto the brush, or just beside it, and it opens its eyes, blinks, and keeps an eye on your pointer until you go away again. This is only for a brush that is resting: one that is sad or nervous keeps its face.
- **Press it.** The brush sinks in where you pressed, and stays in while you hold the button. Let go and it springs back out, a little too far, with a ring of paint spreading from the spot. It also screws its eyes shut while it is pressed, whatever it was doing: a sad or nervous brush squints for as long as you press, then goes back to its face.
- **Press it again and again.** After four presses in about two seconds the brush has had enough: it screws its eyes shut, then glares, goes red, stamps and blows steam, for as long as you keep at it, and calms down once you stop. It is the same in every place, including the welcome window, and afterwards a sad or nervous brush goes back to what it was feeling.

On a phone or tablet a tap is a press. There is nothing to come near with a finger.

## Motion

If your system asks for reduced motion (macOS **Reduce motion**, or Windows **Animation effects** off), the brush still shows its faces but never jumps, shakes, sinks in, looks after your pointer or throws paint.
